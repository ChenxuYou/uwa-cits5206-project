## 3. Marking rubric

**Total: 15 points.** Each criterion has six levels, numbered 0–5 on the LMS card; the
**Points** column is what each level is worth, and the steps between levels are not even.

| Criterion | Points | Weight |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Describes Experience | 7 | 46.67% |
| Feelings and Evaluation | 4 | 26.67% |
| Action Plan | 4 | 26.67% |

### 3.1 Describes Experience

**7 possible points (46.67%)**

| Level | Points | Description |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 0 | 0 | Not addressed. |
| 1 | 1 | Poor identification of a relevant and specific incident, activity or example for reflection. |
| 2 | 2 | Poor identification of a relevant and specific incident, activity or example for reflection. |
| 3 | 3 | Only one example case provided. Simply describes an event that occurred with no attempt to describe why it was selected. |
| 4 | 5 | At least two specific, satisfied examples provided. Provides a clear description with a good analysis of challenging aspects. |
| 5 | 7 | At least three specific, satisfied examples provided. Clear description, with focus on the specific aspects that challenge the student. Offers an insightful and comprehensive analysis, demonstrating deep understanding and critical reflection on challenging aspects. |

### 3.2 Feelings and Evaluation

**4 possible points (26.67%)**

| Level | Points | Description |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 0 | 0 | Not addressed. |
| 1 | 0.5 | Poor interpretation. |
| 2 | 1 | Limited interpretation of what happened or of your response to it. Limited to description of general opinions and behaviours without reflecting on the drivers for those. |
| 3 | 2 | This reflection gives some interpretation of the events that transpired, questions the responses provided, and considers alternative approaches using general examples. |
| 4 | 3 | The reflection clearly details feelings and thoughts experienced during and after the event, including some analysis of why these feelings occurred, with a mention of both positive and negative aspects, but may lack depth in exploring the event's significance or leave some questions insufficiently addressed. Detailed examples are required. |
| 5 | 4 | The reflection provides a comprehensive and insightful account of feelings, thoughts, and reactions, both during the experience and upon reflection. It analyzes the causes of these emotions, thoroughly evaluates the positive and negative aspects, and explicitly discusses the significance of the experience, offering a well-rounded perspective. |

**4 possible points (26.67%)**

| Level | Points | Description |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 0 | 0 | Not addressed. |

| 2 | 1 | Poor action plan in the reflection. |

## 4. Reading the rubric

Our reading, not the unit's words.

| # | What the rubric says | What it means in practice |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **R1** | **Describes Experience is almost half the mark, and it is gated on a count.** One example caps it at 3/7; two unlock 5/7; three are needed for 7/7. The points go 3 → 5 → 7, so there is no 4 or 6 to land on | Use **at least three** specific incidents from the project, and say **why each one was chosen** — level 3 is defined by "no attempt to describe why it was selected" |
| **R2** | **Every top band is about what was *challenging*.** "Focus on the specific aspects that challenge the student" (3.1), the event's "significance" (3.2), "innovative suggestions" (3.3) | For each incident, name what made it hard *for you*, not only what the team did |
| **R3** | **Feelings and Evaluation wants causes, both sides, and significance.** Level 4 asks for "both positive and negative aspects" and states "detailed examples are required"; level 5 adds that it "explicitly discusses the significance" | Say why each feeling arose, what went well as well as what didn't, and why the incident mattered |
| **R4** | **Action Plan is marked on specificity.** Level 3 is "only generalized statements"; level 4 "may lack specific or innovative examples" | Tie each lesson to a concrete thing you will do differently, traceable back to one of the incidents — not "I will communicate better" |

The three criteria follow the usual shape of a reflective cycle: describe what happened, how
it felt and how it went, then what you will do next. Workshop 1's *Listen*, *Speak* and
*Reflect* slides (18–23) quote excerpts from past students' reflections as examples of the
register — see
[`workshop-1-intro-to-cits5206.md` §4](workshop-1-intro-to-cits5206.md#4-communication-listen-speak-reflect).
