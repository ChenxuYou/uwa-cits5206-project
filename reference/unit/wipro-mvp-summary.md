# MVP — Summary

Source: `Wipro-MVP.pdf` (Wipro CSSE Workshop)

## Definition

**MVP = Minimum Viable Product**

> An MVP is a version of a new product which allows a team to collect the maximum amount of validated learning about customers with the least effort.

- **Minimum** — the most rudimentary, bare-bones foundation of the solution possible
- **Viable** — sufficient enough for early adopters
- **Product** — something tangible customers can touch and feel

## How to build one

Build a **vertical slice** through all layers (functional → reliable → usable → emotional design), not one horizontal layer at a time. Deliver a narrow but end-to-end working path.

## Planning steps

1. **Identify business and market needs** — set a long-term goal and define success criteria.
2. **Map out user journeys** — identify the user, their actions (jobs), and the story endings.
3. **Create a pain and gain map** — for each action, list the user's pains and the gains from solving them; find where you add the most value.
4. **Decide what features to build** — ask *want vs. need*. Turn findings into opportunity statements, then rank on a prioritisation matrix (impact × urgency). Only high-impact + high-urgency items go in the MVP; the rest go on the roadmap.

## After the MVP

Collect user feedback → validate against the market → feed new ideas into the next version. Test, learn, measure, repeat — this is the Agile sprint loop (Plan → Design → Develop → Test → Deploy → Review → Launch).
