## 3. Marking Rubric

Two criteria, 20 points total per question (scaled to the 10 marks on the paper).

### Critical Thinking about the Question Scenario — 12 points (60%)

| Level | Pts | Descriptor |
|---|---|---|
| **Excellent** | 12 | Clearly identifies the key issue(s) in the scenario, considers relevant stakeholders or constraints, evaluates alternatives, and justifies a position or recommendation with specific, logical reasons or evidence. |
| **Satisfactory** | 8 | Identifies the main issue and takes a position, but analysis is partial; considers a few reasons or implications, with some justification, yet misses important perspectives, assumptions, or consequences. |
| **Fair** | 4 | Misidentifies or only vaguely states the issue; response is mostly opinion or description with little reasoning; does not consider alternatives, assumptions, or implications in a meaningful way. |

### Clarity and Structure of Communication — 8 points (40%)

| Level | Pts | Descriptor |
|---|---|---|
| **Excellent** | 8 | Response directly answers the question, is logically ordered (e.g. position > reasons > brief conclusion), uses precise language, and is easy to follow with minimal errors that do not affect meaning. |
| **Satisfactory** | 4 | Main point is understandable and generally relevant, but the response may be loosely structured, occasionally repetitive, or have wording and errors that mildly distract from the message. |
| **Fair** | 2 | Response does not clearly answer the question, lacks logical flow, or is so brief/disorganised that the reasoning is difficult to understand; frequent errors or vague language obscure meaning. |

> Note: this is an on-paper test — the rubric is for reference only, there is no LMS submission.

### What the rubric is really rewarding

Four things must be visibly present in every answer to reach *Excellent* on Critical Thinking:

1. **Issue** — name the ethical issue explicitly, don't just describe the situation.
2. **Stakeholders / constraints** — who is affected (client, users, public, teammates, employer), and what limits apply (deadline, budget, contract, skills).
3. **Alternatives** — show you considered at least one other course of action and why you rejected it.
4. **Justified position** — commit to a recommendation and back it with specific reasons, ideally citing the ACS Code.

Merely describing the scenario or giving an opinion caps you at *Fair*.

## 4. Answer Template

The rubric names the structure it wants — use it literally.

> **Position** — "The key issue here is X. I would do Y."
> **Reasons** — "This engages ACS *Trustworthiness* 6 (not undertake work for which you do not have adequate skills), because …"
> **Stakeholders** — "The affected parties are A, B and C; the main constraint is D."
> **Alternatives** — "One could instead do Z, but that would … which is why I reject it."
> **Conclusion** — one sentence restating the recommendation.

Practical notes:

- Write in short paragraphs, not bullet points — it is a "paragraph answers" test.
- Name the value **and** the numbered clause. "This is dishonest" scores far lower than "This breaches *Honesty* 2 — misrepresentation by omission."
- Always commit to a position. Sitting on the fence loses the "justifies a position" marks.
- Legible handwriting and clean structure are worth 40% — leave a line between paragraphs.

## 5. ACS Code of Professional Ethics (March 2023)

Printed at the end of the exam paper, but know it cold so you are not reading it under time pressure.

### Core values

**Honesty · Trustworthiness · Respect for Others · Respect for the Profession**

### Honesty

1. Be honest, open and truthful in all interactions with employers, employees, clients, stakeholders, and the public.
2. Not misrepresent any action, situation or capability — of yourself, colleagues, employers, or others — whether directly, indirectly, **or by omission**.
3. **Not remain silent when you detect unprofessional conduct.**

### Trustworthiness

1. Be accountable for all you undertake, and take responsibility for failures as well as successes.
2. Practise integrity; be consistent in views, words and actions, and **declare and manage conflicts of interest**.
3. Engage in ongoing professional development to remain current with ICT knowledge, skills, and standards.
4. **Respect the privacy, confidentiality, and integrity** of personal or proprietary information.
5. Communicate your own capabilities clearly, including any learning or skills gaps.
6. **Not undertake work for which you do not have adequate skills or knowledge.**
7. Be competent in everything you undertake, striving for quality and highlighting relevant constraints.
8. Develop systems that are robust, secure, and user-friendly.

### Respect for Others

Minimise harm to stakeholders, and:

1. Develop **mitigation strategies where harm is unavoidable**.
2. Be respectful of differing views and opinions.
3. Be impartial and fair, and avoid unfair discrimination.
4. Model and encourage inclusivity in all professional work.
5. Support and contribute to a healthy, respectful workplace.
6. Identify and mitigate risks to others arising from your work.
7. **Respect the intellectual property of others.**

### Respect for the Profession

1. Help educate the public about the benefits and drawbacks of ICT systems.
2. Contribute to public discussion on ICT issues affecting dignity and wellbeing.
3. Promote environmentally sustainable ICT and **defend the public interest**.
4. Encourage and support the professional development of others.
5. Contribute to advancing ICT capabilities for the greater good.
6. Acknowledge and help address challenges facing the ICT industry.
7. Support an open, diverse, and inclusive ICT profession.
8. Promote collective ICT knowledge and the standing of the profession and the ACS.

### Fast mapping — scenario cue → clause

| If the scenario involves … | Cite |
|---|---|
| Hiding a defect, staying quiet about a problem | Honesty 2 (omission), Honesty 3 |
| Overstating what the team can deliver | Honesty 2, Trustworthiness 5 |
| Accepting work beyond the team's skills | Trustworthiness 6, 5, 7 |
| Personal gain, side deals, competing loyalties | Trustworthiness 2 |
| User data, personal records, client confidentiality | Trustworthiness 4 |
| Insecure or fragile system shipped anyway | Trustworthiness 8, Respect for Others 6 |
| Copying code, unlicensed libraries, unattributed GenAI output | Respect for Others 7 |
| A feature that harms or excludes some users | Respect for Others 1, 3, 4, 6 |
| Environmental cost, wider public impact | Respect for the Profession 3 |
| A teammate being ignored, bullied, or overloaded | Respect for Others 2, 5 |

## 6. Q4 — Group Project Question

Q4 is explicitly "a question related to your group project", drawn from workshop content: Listen / Speak / Reflect, the Team Code of Conduct, individual accountability, professional emails, "it is OK to say no", and client communication (listen for **what** the client wants, don't jump to **how**).

### Likely question angles

- What is in your team's Code of Conduct, and how has it actually been used?
- Describe a disagreement or conflict in your team — how was it resolved, and what would you do differently?
- Describe a client interaction — how did you confirm you had understood the requirement correctly?
- How did your team handle a request that was beyond its skills or outside scope?
- How does your team handle reused code, external libraries, or GenAI assistance?
- How is individual accountability tracked, and is the workload fair?

### Preparation

For each angle above, prepare **one concrete example from the project in two sentences** — what happened, and what you concluded. Generic answers about "good communication" score *Fair*; a named incident with a named outcome scores *Excellent*.

Material available in this repo to mine for examples:

- `docs/client/` — the five open questions, the client's written answers, the signed scope summary
- `docs/meetings/` — kickoff, weekly team meetings, 20 Aug client meeting
- `docs/project/risks.md`, `skills-audit.md`, `plan.md`
- `docs/decisions/adr-001-technology-stack.md`
- `reference/unit/individual-accountability.docx`, `teamroles.docx`
